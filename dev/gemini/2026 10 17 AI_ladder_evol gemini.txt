Ladder Literature ReviewBottom-Line Summary: Existing AI ladders fall into three distinct traditions: frontier lab capability roadmaps, enterprise maturity and autonomy scales, and developer control-flow hierarchies. Theory vs. Practice: Lab roadmaps frequently blend current product usage with speculative future thresholds such as autonomous corporations, whereas practitioner frameworks classify real-world software architectures deployed in production. Control-Flow Consensus: By late 2024, industry literature converged on a sharp architectural boundary separating fixed multi-step code paths (workflows) from model-directed dynamic execution loops (agents).Software Shift Evolution: Former Tesla AI Director and OpenAI researcher Andrej Karpathy established the foundational developer ordering by dividing computing history into three eras. Explicit Code (Software 1.0): Traditional engineering relies on human programmers writing explicit rules in languages such as C++, Java, or Python to process data predictably. Learned Weights (Software 2.0): Machine learning replaces human-written rules with optimization over curated datasets to tune millions or billions of numerical parameters (neural network weights) for specialized tasks like image recognition or translation. Natural Language (Software 3.0): Large language models (LLMs) treat plain English instructions and structured text memory windows (context windows) as programmable operating systems, shifting developer effort from weight optimization to context orchestration.OpenAI AGI Roadmap: In July 2024, OpenAI disclosed an internal five-tier classification scale designed to measure progress toward human-level general capability (artificial general intelligence / AGI) by tracking how much human direction a system removes from the loop. Five Rung Progression: The hierarchy ascends from conversational systems that respond to user prompts (Level 1: Chatbots), to models capable of PhD-level problem solving without external tools (Level 2: Reasoners), to systems that execute multi-day tasks across external software (Level 3: Agents), to systems generating novel scientific discoveries (Level 4: Innovators), and finally to systems managing entire corporate operations (Level 5: Organizations). Real-World Calibration: Empirical audits of enterprise deployments through 2026 demonstrate that industry usage sits firmly around Level 2.5, where step-by-step reasoning tasks are routine, Level 3 agent deployments succeed in verifiable domains like software engineering but face high cancellation rates in unstructured business operations, and Levels 4 and 5 remain completely theoretical.DeepMind Matrix Classification: Researchers at Google DeepMind (Morris et al., published November 2023 and updated through 2025) rejected single-axis ladders in favor of a two-dimensional grid separating capability depth (performance) from task breadth (generality), paired with a distinct six-level scale for human-AI interaction (autonomy). Performance Rungs: DeepMind ranks performance against skilled human adults across six tiers: Level 0 (No AI), Level 1 (Emerging: equal to an unskilled human), Level 2 (Competent: 50th percentile of skilled adults), Level 3 (Expert: 90th percentile), Level 4 (Virtuoso / Exceptional: 99th percentile), and Level 5 (Superhuman: outperforming 100% of humans, exemplified in narrow tasks by protein-folding system AlphaFold). Autonomy Decoupling: Crucially, Morris et al. demonstrated that model capability unlocks—but does not dictate—the deployed autonomy level, which spans Level 0 (human does everything), Level 1 (AI as a Tool), Level 2 (AI as a Consultant invoked by humans), Level 3 (AI as a Collaborator), Level 4 (AI as an Expert driving interaction), and Level 5 (AI as an Agent operating with full independence).Anthropic Architectural Split: In December 2024, Anthropic engineers Erik Schluntz and Barry Zhang published Building Effective Agents, establishing the definitive engineering classification used by production teams. Workflows vs. Agents: Anthropic grouped all multi-step systems under the umbrella of action-taking systems (agentic systems) but drew a strict boundary between predefined code orchestration (workflows) and model-driven process control (agents). Five Workflow Patterns: Under predefined workflows, Anthropic cataloged five composable structures: (1) the augmented LLM (base model plus document search, tools, and memory), (2) prompt chaining (sequential calls with programmatic gate checks), (3) routing (classifying inputs to specialized prompts or smaller models), (4) parallelization (splitting tasks via sectioning or running multiple suitability checks via voting), and (5) orchestrator-workers (a central model dynamically delegating sub-tasks to worker models). Autonomous Loops: True agents occupy the top tier, where the model independently plans, selects abstract tools such as terminal execution (bash) or file modification (edit), inspects environmental feedback, and loops until verification criteria pass.Enterprise & Autonomy Scales: Venture capital firms, industry analysts, and framework creators constructed parallel ladders focused on commercial adoption and control transfer. SAE Coding Adaptation: Engineering teams adapted the automotive industry's self-driving car levels (SAE L0–L5) to software engineering: L0 (manual coding), L1 (single-line ghost-text autocomplete), L2 (chat-assisted function drafting), L3 (conditional multi-file editing under "plan, approve, execute" human supervision), L4 (background autonomous pull-request resolution in sandboxed test environments), and L5 (unsupervised full-lifecycle software engineering). Resource MetricsBottom-Line Summary: Measuring the cost of climbing an AI ladder requires five complementary units—text chunks (tokens), compute operations (FLOPs), electrical energy (Wh), monetary expenditure (USD), and human-vs-AI duration—because no single metric captures both hardware physics and workplace economics. Runtime Compute Crossover: While pre-training compute for frontier models grew 5-fold per year (0.7 orders of magnitude annually) from $10^{22}$ FLOPs in 2020 to $10^{26}+$ FLOPs in 2025, runtime inference scaling allows practitioners to multiply single-task compute by 100 to 1,000,000 times ($2$ to $6$ orders of magnitude) on verifiable math and coding tasks. Three-Tier Calculation Rule: Whenever direct per-step disclosures are absent, resource estimates combine published model parameter counts and API price tables with standardized physical equations linking active parameters to forward-pass FLOPs and datacenter power draw.Unit Trade-Off Comparison: Each candidate measurement unit exhibits specific strengths and blind spots across historical eras. Text Chunks (Tokens): Tokens (~0.75 English words per chunk) directly govern API billing and context memory limits after June 2020, jumping from $50$ tokens for a single line of code autocomplete to $1,200,000+$ tokens for a multi-agent research run, but were not standard billing units for pre-2020 statistical classifiers. Compute Operations (FLOPs): Floating-point operations provide an era-agnostic physical measure of processor work across both 2018 CPUs/GPUs and 2026 AI clusters, governed during deployment by the dense transformer forward-pass approximation:
$$\text{C}_{\text{deployment}} \approx 2 \times N_{\text{active}} \times T_{\text{total}}$$
where $N_{\text{active}}$ represents active model parameters per token and $T_{\text{total}}$ represents total tokens processed (adjusting for key-value memory caching [KV-cache] and sparse mixture-of-experts [MoE] routing). Electrical Energy (Wh): Watt-hours translate compute into physical grid demand, anchored by empirical measurements such as Luccioni et al. (2022) recording $\sim 4\text{ Wh}$ per query on the 176-billion-parameter BLOOM model, and hardware efficiency gains where GPU FLOP/s per watt improved by 34% per year ($1.34\times$/year) since 2023. Monetary Cost (USD): Dollar figures capture real commercial friction—reflecting both a 49% annual increase in chip performance per dollar ($1.49\times$/year) and a 50% to 90% discount on cached input tokens—and must be normalized across pay-per-token API tiers and $20–$200/month flat-rate consumer subscriptions. Human-vs-AI Duration: Tracking human prompting and verification minutes against autonomous AI execution seconds reveals the crucial workplace shift from human-in-the-loop typing to background task delegation.Compute & Price Trajectory: Historical data from Epoch AI and provider pricing tables demonstrate how rising base capability paired with plummeting marginal execution cost to unlock multi-step agent ladders. Part II — Applied Ladder-Building MethodBottom-Line Summary: Constructing an objective historical sequence of AI ladders requires enforcing strict era transition rules, pairing bottom-up workflow clustering with top-down theoretical pruning, and verifying every step split against a quantitative three-variable test. Five Locked Eras: Applying the two-shift structural threshold across the 2018–2026 historical archive locks exactly five chronological Era Ladders ($L_1$ through $L_5$). Speculative Step Pruning: Theoretical rungs lacking real-world adoption data—specifically OpenAI's Level 4 ("Innovators") and Level 5 ("Organizations") and SAE Level 5 ("Fully Unsupervised Software Engineering")—are set aside from the historical ladders to preserve factual accuracy.2.1. Two-Shift Era Thresholds & Boundary DecisionsBottom-Line Summary: A new Era Ladder ($L_t$) begins only when a technical breakthrough or 10-fold price reduction triggers at least two simultaneous structural changes across the ladder steps. Avoiding Model Churn: Minor incremental checkpoint releases (such as moving from GPT-3.5 to GPT-3.5-turbo-0613 or Claude 3.5 Sonnet to Claude 3.7) are absorbed within their existing era rather than spawning fragmented ladders. Five Structural Inflections: Between October 2018 and October 2026, exactly four watershed moments satisfied the multi-shift threshold, dividing the eight-year history into five distinct ladders.Threshold Application Decisions: Each era boundary reflects a verified simultaneous collapse of an old step and birth of a new upper tier. Cutoff 1 (2020-06 — Launch of OpenAI GPT-3 API): Transitioned the industry from $L_1$ (training task-specific encoders like BERT) to $L_2$ (prompting foundation completion models), simultaneously collapsing feature-engineered statistical classifiers and introducing zero-shot/few-shot in-context learning plus inline code autocomplete (Codex). Cutoff 2 (2022-11 — Release of ChatGPT & gpt-3.5-turbo): Combined a 10-fold API price drop (from $20.00/1M tokens on text-davinci-003 to $2.00/1M tokens on gpt-3.5-turbo) with dialogue tuning, sinking raw zero-shot completion into a universal consumer web chat UI (Step 1 of $L_3$) while spawning custom vector-database document search (retrieval-augmented generation / RAG, Step 3) and unconstrained AutoGPT agent loops (Step 5). Cutoff 3 (2023-11 — GPT-4 Turbo 128k & Claude 2.1 200k Context): Sunk basic single-file RAG into default chat file-upload buttons (Step 1 of $L_4$), eliminated brittle unconstrained AutoGPT loops in favor of predefined tool-calling and state-machine harnesses (LangGraph, Step 5), and split coding into multi-file code editors (Cursor, Step 4). Cutoff 4 (2024-09 to 2024-11 — OpenAI o1-preview, Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2 Computer Use & MCP): Introduced training models to think step by step before answering (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards / RLVR test-time compute, Step 2 of $L_5$), standardized tool connections via the Model Context Protocol (MCP, Step 3), and elevated autonomous command-line coding (Claude Code, Step 4) and graphical screen-clicking agents (Step 5) into production reality.2.2. Pruning Speculative Tiers: Top-down cross-checking removed theoretical autonomy tiers that generated zero verified usage in enterprise audits.Empirical Workflow Grouping: In Pass 1, concrete workflows taught in DeepLearning.AI courses, Stanford CS324, and Anthropic cookbooks were grouped by their technical setup and execution loop. Part III — Chronological Ladder EvolutionBottom-Line Summary: Across the eight-year span from October 2018 to October 2026, the AI usage ladder reorganized five times as foundation model parameters scaled from $10^8$ to $10^{12}+$, context windows expanded 4,000-fold from 512 tokens to 2,000,000 tokens, and single-task agentic compute surged by 11 orders of magnitude ($10^6$ FLOPs to $9 \times 10^{16}$ FLOPs). Training to Prompting to Acting: Practitioners progressed from training separate neural network weights for every narrow classification task ($L_1$), to prompting a single frozen foundation model via text completion ($L_2$), to wiring external vector databases and early agent loops ($L_3$), to embedding multimodal long-context models inside code editors and state-machine workflows ($L_4$), and finally to delegating multi-hour engineering and research tasks to reasoning models and protocol-standardized command-line and desktop agents ($L_5$).3.1. Era 1 Ladder (L1: 2018-10 to 2020-05) — Task-Specific Representation & Fine-TuningBottom-Line Summary: During Era 1 (2018-10 to 2020-05), applying AI to text required data science teams to collect labeled datasets and train or fine-tune a separate model for every individual business task. Transformer Transfer Breakthrough: Google's October 2018 release of bidirectional transformer encoders (BERT) replaced complex recurrent neural networks at the top of the ladder with pre-trained encoders fine-tuned on single cloud GPUs. Zero-Prompting Era: Because models lacked generative instruction-following capabilities, 80% of industry practitioners relied on deterministic pattern rules (regular expressions) and tabular statistical classifiers (scikit-learn), while only 5% of users operated at the top fine-tuning tier.Valid Dates & Why Old Ladder Got OutdatedEra Window (2018-10 to 2020-05): Era 1 opened in October 2018 when Google researchers published BERT and HuggingFace released its open-source PyTorch library (pytorch-pretrained-bert, later renamed transformers), establishing pre-train-then-fine-tune as the gold standard for natural language processing (NLP). Outdating Pre-2018 Architectures: Prior to October 2018, NLP ladders required building custom sequential recurrent networks with long short-term memory cells (BiLSTM) from scratch on top of static word lookup tables (Word2Vec or GloVe), which failed to capture word meaning changes across sentence contexts. Why L1 Ended in May 2020: Era 1 became outdated in May–June 2020 when OpenAI published the 175-billion-parameter next-word prediction model (GPT-3) and launched its commercial completion API, proving that a single massive model could solve translation, classification, and drafting via plain-text examples in the prompt (in-context learning) without updating a single neural network weight.How Steps ChangedStructural Realignment: Compared to pre-2018 NLP workflows, $L_1$ retained deterministic rules at Step 1 and classical statistical classifiers at Step 2, compressed static word embeddings and recurrent networks into Step 3, and introduced New Top Step 4 (Fine-Tuned Transformer Encoders) where transfer learning on 110M–340M parameter models (BERT-base and BERT-large) achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on classification and extraction benchmarks.Step-by-Step Breakdown (Steps 1 to 4)Step 1 — Deterministic Rules & Regular Expressions (L1-P1): Step Boundaries: Step 1 represents the lowest task complexity floor (single pattern or keyword extraction), requiring zero training data or statistical inference whereas Step 2 requires labeled training examples and feature matrices, and failing completely when phrasing varies outside explicit human-coded patterns (the Software 1.0 limit). Concrete Tool Stack: Practitioners relied on the Python standard library re, spaCy rule-based Matcher and PhraseMatcher, NLTK tokenizers, Elasticsearch keyword analyzers, and SQL LIKE queries running on standard CPUs. Use Cases & Context Switching: Software engineers and data analysts extracted dates, invoice numbers, email addresses, and profanity filters from server logs and customer forms; a practitioner used Step 1 regex for structured fields (such as phone numbers) before switching to Step 2 or Step 4 for unstructured sentiment or customer intent. Step Resource Use: A single task uses $0$ neural tokens, $\sim 10^6\text{ FLOPs}$ of CPU string matching, $\$0.000001\text{ USD}$, $0.00001\text{ Wh}$ of electricity, and $15\text{ minutes}$ of human regex crafting versus $0.001\text{ seconds}$ of runtime, while capturing $50\%$ of active NLP practitioners, $0\%$ of neural token volume, and $10\%$ of total era spend.Step 2 — Feature-Engineered Statistical Classifiers (L1-P2): Step Boundaries: Step 2 moves from rigid string rules to statistical document classification across 100–5,000 word documents by converting text into word-frequency counts (bag-of-words / TF-IDF), but lacks sequential word-order awareness and fails when negation or word order flips meaning, which requires Step 3 or Step 4. Concrete Tool Stack: Data teams deployed scikit-learn (TfidfVectorizer, LogisticRegression, MultinomialNB, LinearSVC), XGBoost, LightGBM, and Pandas running in Jupyter Notebooks on local laptops or AWS EC2 CPU instances. Use Cases & Context Switching: Enterprise data scientists built spam filters, support ticket routers, and churn predictors taught in foundational Coursera and Fast.ai machine learning modules; practitioners always ran Step 2 as a fast baseline before justifying the GPU cost of Step 3 or Step 4. Step Resource Use: A single task inference uses $0$ neural tokens, $\sim 10^8\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.00001\text{ USD}$, $0.0001\text{ Wh}$, and $30\text{ minutes}$ of human feature engineering and labeling versus $0.05\text{ seconds}$ of execution, representing $30\%$ of users, $0\%$ of neural tokens, and $25\%$ of era spend.Step 3 — Static Embeddings & Recurrent Sequences (L1-P3): Step Boundaries: Step 3 upgrades from word-count tables to dense numerical word vectors (300-dimensional embeddings) passed through sequential recurrent layers (BiLSTM) that track word order for word-by-word entity tagging (named entity recognition / NER) and machine translation, but is limited by memory decay over long paragraphs and an inability to distinguish words with multiple meanings (like "bank") across contexts, which requires Step 4 bidirectional attention. Concrete Tool Stack: Engineers combined pre-trained Word2Vec (Google), GloVe (Stanford), fastText (Facebook), and spaCy v2 pipelines with custom PyTorch or TensorFlow 1.x BiLSTM-CRF architectures trained on NVIDIA K80 or P100 GPUs. Use Cases & Context Switching: NLP engineers highlighted person, organization, and medical drug names in clinical notes and e-commerce search queries (core assignments in 2018–2019 Stanford CS224N); teams switched from Step 2 to Step 3 when word sequence mattered but latency budgets prohibited heavy transformer layers. Step Resource Use: A single task processes $\sim 200$ word or subword tokens, consuming $\sim 10^{10}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.0001\text{ USD}$, $0.002\text{ Wh}$, and $20\text{ minutes}$ of human pipeline tuning versus $0.2\text{ seconds}$ of runtime, accounting for $15\%$ of users, $20\%$ of neural token volume, and $30\%$ of era spend.Step 4 — Fine-Tuned Transformer Encoders (L1-P4): Step Boundaries: Step 4 represents the Era 1 complexity ceiling—adapting all 110M–340M parameters of a pre-trained bidirectional transformer (BERT) plus a custom task classification head on 1,000–100,000 labeled examples—but is strictly capped at 512 subword tokens and incapable of open-ended text generation or zero-shot task switching without retraining. Concrete Tool Stack: Production stacks centered on HuggingFace transformers (bert-base-uncased, roberta-large, distilbert-base-uncased), Google Cloud TPUs or AWS p3.2xlarge instances (NVIDIA V100 16GB GPUs), the PyTorch AdamW optimizer, and ONNX Runtime for quantized serving. Use Cases & Context Switching: Search engine teams (such as Google Search adopting BERT in late 2019), legal contract reviewers, and biomedical researchers used Step 4 for extractive question answering (SQuAD) and semantic similarity, reserving it for high-value production endpoints after Step 1–3 baselines plateaued. Step Resource Use: A single inference task processes $256$ subword tokens (capped at 512), requiring $\sim 1.7 \times 10^{11}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.001\text{ USD}$ (including amortized GPU fine-tuning), $0.05\text{ Wh}$, and $45\text{ minutes}$ of dataset curation and training monitoring versus $0.5\text{ seconds}$ of inference, serving $5\%$ of practitioners while driving $80\%$ of transformer token volume and $35\%$ of era spend.Full-Ladder Resource Totals (L1)Aggregate Era Footprint (2018-10 to 2020-05): Across its 20-month window, $L_1$ encompassed approximately $1.5\text{ million}$ active ML/NLP developers and data scientists globally, logging $\sim 450\text{ million}$ human engineering hours. Total era compute across training (such as BERT-large at $\sim 10^{20}\text{ FLOPs}$ and T5-11B at $\sim 10^{22}\text{ FLOPs}$) and deployment inference reached $\sim 5 \times 10^{23}\text{ FLOPs}$, consuming $\sim 85,000\text{ MWh}$ of datacenter electricity, $\sim 1.2\text{ trillion}$ subword tokens, and $\sim \$450\text{ million}$ in enterprise cloud GPU and NLP software expenditure.3.2. Era 2 Ladder (L2: 2020-06 to 2022-11) — Foundation Completion & In-Context PromptingBottom-Line Summary: Era 2 (2020-06 to 2022-11) inverted the AI software stack by replacing per-task model training with natural language prompting of a single massive foundation model (GPT-3 and Codex). Birth of Inline Coding: The June 2021 launch of GitHub Copilot introduced real-time ghost-text code autocomplete inside code editors as the new Step 1 entry point, capturing 55% of active users. High-Cost Completion Barrier: Because flagship davinci API calls cost $20.00 to $60.00 per million tokens and required carefully formatted text-completion prefixes rather than conversational dialogue, usage remained restricted to developers, copywriting startups, and enterprise ML teams.Valid Dates & Why Old Ladder Got OutdatedEra Window (2020-06 to 2022-11): Era 2 began in June 2020 when OpenAI opened commercial API access to GPT-3 (davinci, curie, babbage, ada), followed in June 2021 by the GitHub Copilot technical preview powered by OpenAI's 12B-parameter Codex model, and in January 2022 by InstructGPT (text-davinci-001 through 003) trained with human preference feedback (reinforcement learning from human feedback / RLHF). Why L1 Got Outdated: Stanford's Center for Research on Foundation Models (CRFM) and course CS324 documented that a single 175B-parameter model prompted with three text examples (few-shot learning) matched or beat fine-tuned BERT models across dozens of tasks without requiring labeled training sets or dedicated GPU servers. Why L2 Ended in November 2022: On November 30, 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo), which wrapped dialogue state management into a free consumer web interface and slashed API token pricing 10-fold from $20.00/1M tokens (text-davinci-003) to $2.00/1M tokens, instantly rendering raw text-completion prompting and standalone copywriting wrappers outdated.How Steps ChangedCross-Era Step Transitions: Between $L_1$ and $L_2$, three major structural shifts occurred across the ladder. Combined Legacy Steps: $L_1$ Steps 1, 2, and 3 (Regex, TF-IDF classifiers, and BiLSTMs) were displaced from active AI application roadmaps as a single GPT-3 completion call handled classification, entity extraction, and sentiment simultaneously into Zero-Shot Prompt Completion (L2-P2) and Few-Shot In-Context Demonstration Chains (L2-P3), establishing natural language programming (Software 3.0) as the core practitioner workflow. New Bottom Entry Step: Inline Ghost-Text Code Autocomplete (L2-P1) emerged at the bottom of the ladder via GitHub Copilot in VS Code, giving millions of software developers low-latency 50-token predictions without leaving their code editor. Evolved Top Step: $L_1$'s BERT encoder fine-tuning evolved into Instruction Fine-Tuning & Task-Specific Head Tuning (L2-P4) using sequence-to-sequence models (T5, BART) and OpenAI's Fine-Tuning API on curie and davinci.Step-by-Step Breakdown (Steps 1 to 4)Step 1 — Inline Ghost-Text Code Autocomplete (L2-P1): Step Boundaries: Step 1 is the lowest complexity step in $L_2$, predicting 1 to 3 lines of code (~50 tokens) automatically from cursor context without explicit prompt writing, whereas Step 2 requires formulating natural language instructions, and Step 1 fails on whole-function architecture or cross-file logic due to narrow local context (~1,000–2,000 tokens) and 300-millisecond latency caps. Concrete Tool Stack: Developers installed the GitHub Copilot extension inside Visual Studio Code, JetBrains IDEs, and Neovim, powered by OpenAI Codex (code-cushman-001 12B and code-davinci-002), alongside TabNine. Use Cases & Context Switching: Software engineers auto-completed boilerplate loops, unit test assertions, and SQL queries; a developer used Step 1 continuously while typing code, then switched to the browser Playground at Step 2 or Step 3 when stuck on an unfamiliar algorithm or regex pattern. Step Resource Use: A single task processes $\sim 50$ tokens, $\sim 1.2 \times 10^{12}\text{ FLOPs}$ ($2 \times 12\text{B} \times 50$), $\$0.0005\text{ USD}$ (prorated across a $10/month subscription), $0.02\text{ Wh}$, and $0.2\text{ minutes}$ (12 seconds) of human review versus $0.3\text{ seconds}$ of AI generation, capturing $55\%$ of active era users, $25\%$ of tokens, and $20\%$ of era spend.Step 2 — Zero-Shot Prompt Completion & Text Transformation (L2-P2): Step Boundaries: Step 2 moves from passive code autocomplete to explicit natural language instructions for single-paragraph drafting, summarization, or classification (~300 tokens), differing from Step 1 by requiring deliberate prompt design and from Step 3 by omitting in-context input-output examples, while breaking down when tasks require strict output schemas (like nested JSON) or multi-step logic. Concrete Tool Stack: Practitioners used the OpenAI Playground web interface, the OpenAI Completion API (text-davinci-001, text-davinci-002, text-davinci-003, text-curie-001), and early commercial SaaS wrappers including Jasper.ai, Copy.ai, and AI Dungeon. Use Cases & Context Switching: Marketing copywriters generated ad variations, product managers summarized meeting notes, and early adopters explored InstructGPT; users started with a zero-shot prompt at Step 2 and escalated to Step 3 few-shot examples whenever davinci broke the output format. Step Resource Use: A single task uses $\sim 300$ tokens, $\sim 1.05 \times 10^{14}\text{ FLOPs}$ ($2 \times 175\text{B} \times 300$), $\$0.018\text{ USD}$ (at $60/1M tokens on davinci or $0.006 at $20/1M on davinci-003), $1.5\text{ Wh}$, and $3\text{ minutes}$ of human prompt tweaking versus $2.0\text{ seconds}$ of AI runtime, representing $25\%$ of users, $30\%$ of tokens, and $35\%$ of era spend.Step 3 — Few-Shot In-Context Demonstration Chains (L2-P3): Step Boundaries: Step 3 supplies 3 to 10 curated input-output pairs (~1,200 tokens) plus explicit step-by-step reasoning cues ("Let's think step by step", Kojima and Wei et al. 2022) inside the 2,048-to-4,096 token context window, overcoming Step 2 formatting and logic failures without altering weights, but hitting a hard wall at the 4,096-token context ceiling and incurring $4\times$ higher per-call API bills. Concrete Tool Stack: Production teams called the OpenAI text-davinci-002 and text-davinci-003 API with custom stop sequences (stop=["\n\n", "Input:"]), Cohere Generate API, AI21 Labs Jurassic-1, and early Python prompt-template scripts taught in Stanford CS324 (Winter 2022). Use Cases & Context Switching: AI engineers extracted structured JSON records from unstructured medical or financial text, translated natural language into complex SQL queries, and solved grade-school math word problems (GSM8K); engineers used Step 3 for production API pipelines where Step 2 zero-shot accuracy was below 70%, escalating to Step 4 fine-tuning only when few-shot examples exhausted the 4k context window or cost too much per call. Step Resource Use: A single task processes $\sim 1,200$ tokens, $\sim 4.2 \times 10^{14}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.072\text{ USD}$, $6.0\text{ Wh}$, and $10\text{ minutes}$ of human example curation versus $5.0\text{ seconds}$ of runtime, accounting for $15\%$ of users, $30\%$ of tokens, and $30\%$ of era spend.Step 4 — Instruction Fine-Tuning & Task-Specific Head Tuning (L2-P4): Step Boundaries: Step 4 is the highest complexity step in $L_2$, updating the weights of an open-source encoder-decoder (T5, BART) or running OpenAI's supervised Fine-Tuning API on thousands of JSONL prompt-completion pairs to eliminate the repeated token cost of Step 3 few-shot examples during inference and lock in domain tone, while requiring high upfront engineering effort, GPU clusters, and dataset maintenance. Concrete Tool Stack: ML engineers used the OpenAI Fine-Tuning CLI (openai api fine_tunes.create on curie and davinci), HuggingFace Seq2SeqTrainer with google/flan-t5-xl and facebook/bart-large, DeepSpeed ZeRO, and AWS SageMaker multi-GPU instances. Use Cases & Context Switching: Enterprise ML teams at legal-tech, e-commerce, and healthcare firms built high-volume proprietary classifiers or specialized customer-support responders where passing 1,500 few-shot tokens on every Step 3 call was economically unviable. Step Resource Use: A single task (amortizing fine-tuning compute across deployment runs) averages $\sim 1,000$ tokens, $\sim 2.0 \times 10^{15}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.25\text{ USD}$, $30.0\text{ Wh}$, and $60\text{ minutes}$ of human data preparation versus $20.0\text{ seconds}$ of amortized GPU execution, serving $5\%$ of users while consuming $15\%$ of tokens and $15\%$ of era spend.Full-Ladder Resource Totals (L2)Aggregate Era Footprint (2020-06 to 2022-11): Across its 30-month window, $L_2$ expanded the active practitioner base to $\sim 5\text{ million}$ users (driven by 1.2M+ early GitHub Copilot users and OpenAI Playground developers), logging $\sim 900\text{ million}$ human hours. Total era compute across training (GPT-3 at $3.14 \times 10^{23}\text{ FLOPs}$, PaLM-540B at $2.5 \times 10^{24}\text{ FLOPs}$, Chinchilla) and inference reached $\sim 1.8 \times 10^{25}\text{ FLOPs}$, consuming $\sim 320,000\text{ MWh}$ of electricity, $\sim 45\text{ billion}$ API and autocomplete tokens, and $\sim \$1.4\text{ billion}$ in combined API, cloud compute, and early Copilot and SaaS spend.3.3. Era 3 Ladder (L3: 2022-11 to 2023-11) — Conversational Chat, Custom RAG & Unconstrained Agent LoopsBottom-Line Summary: Era 3 (2022-11 to 2023-11) brought generative AI to 100 million+ mainstream consumers at Step 1 while touching off a massive developer race to overcome narrow 4,096-to-8,192 token context windows using external vector databases (Step 3) and experimental autonomous loops (Step 5). Custom Build Mania: Because web chatbots in early 2023 could not read private corporate PDFs or execute reliable multi-step actions natively, enterprises built nearly half of their solutions internally using Python orchestration libraries (LangChain and LlamaIndex). Brittle Autonomy Ceiling: The era's new top step—unconstrained reason-and-act (ReAct) agent loops exemplified by AutoGPT and BabyAGI—consumed $45,000+$ tokens per run ($1.80 USD) but suffered compounding hallucination loops that prevented production deployment.Valid Dates & Why Old Ladder Got OutdatedEra Window (2022-11 to 2023-11): Era 3 opened on November 30, 2022 with the launch of ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo), accelerated in March 2023 with GPT-4 (8k/32k context) and Anthropic's Claude 1.3, expanded into open-weight models in July 2023 with Meta's Llama-2 (7B/13B/70B) and September 2023 with Mistral-7B, and closed in November 2023 at OpenAI DevDay. Why L2 Got Outdated: gpt-3.5-turbo priced tokens at $2.00/1M ($10\times$ cheaper than text-davinci-003) and replaced awkward text-completion stop sequences with a multi-turn chat interface (system, user, and assistant message arrays), instantly sinking $L_2$'s zero-shot and few-shot completion steps into accessible everyday chat. Why L3 Ended in November 2023: In November 2023, OpenAI released GPT-4 Turbo (128k context window, native multi-function calling, JSON mode, and the Assistants/Custom GPTs file-upload API) alongside Anthropic's Claude 2.1 (200k context), which absorbed custom single-document RAG pipelines directly into Step 1 consumer chat and forced developers to abandon unconstrained AutoGPT loops for structured workflow graphs.How Steps ChangedCross-Era Step Transitions: Five structural shifts reorganized $L_2$ into $L_3$. Sinking into Web Chat: $L_2$'s Step 2 (Zero-Shot Completion) and basic Step 3 (Few-Shot Prompting) sunk into Step 1 (Consumer Web Chat & Conversational Assistance, L3-P1), where non-technical office workers iterated via multi-turn browser dialogue. Template Chaining Evolution: $L_2$'s complex in-context chains formalized into Structured Prompt Engineering & Template Chaining (L3-P2) using programmatic system prompts, output parsers, and sequential chains. Birth of Custom RAG: Custom Retrieval-Augmented Generation (L3-P3) emerged as the signature enterprise engineering tier to bypass 4k–8k context limits by embedding private text chunks into vector databases. Adapter Fine-Tuning Shift: Full-weight fine-tuning transformed into Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT / LoRA, L3-P4) on open-weight Llama-2 and Mistral models using 4-bit quantization (QLoRA). New Agent Apex: Autonomous ReAct Loops & Unconstrained Agents (L3-P5) appeared at the top of the ladder (AutoGPT, BabyAGI), testing whether an LLM could loop indefinitely over goals without human intervention.Step-by-Step Breakdown (Steps 1 to 5)Step 1 — Consumer Web Chat & Conversational Assistance (L3-P1): Step Boundaries: Step 1 is the baseline interactive dialogue tier (~800 tokens per turn) inside a browser window, requiring zero coding or API keys unlike Step 2 programmatic integration, but blocked from reading private multi-document repositories (requiring Step 3 RAG) due to 4,096–8,192 token context cutoffs and a lack of native file-upload parsers in early 2023. Concrete Tool Stack: Everyday users accessed the ChatGPT free web UI (gpt-3.5-turbo) and ChatGPT Plus ($20/month GPT-4 8k), Anthropic Claude 1.3 and Claude 2.0 web chat, Microsoft Bing Chat (Sydney), and Google Bard. Use Cases & Context Switching: Knowledge workers drafted emails, students summarized pasted article excerpts, and software developers copy-pasted stack traces into a browser tab (documented in the 2023 Stack Overflow Developer Survey and DeepMind's placement of ChatGPT and Bard at Emerging AGI); a developer used Step 1 for ad-hoc syntax questions before switching to Step 2 or Step 3 when building an automated software feature. Step Resource Use: A single task uses $\sim 800$ tokens, $\sim 3.2 \times 10^{13}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.0016\text{ USD}$ (at the gpt-3.5-turbo $2/1M rate), $0.3\text{ Wh}$, and $2\text{ minutes}$ of human typing and reading versus $2.5\text{ seconds}$ of AI streaming, capturing $65\%$ of era users, $50\%$ of global token traffic, and $40\%$ of era spend.Step 2 — Structured Prompt Engineering & Template Chaining (L3-P2): Step Boundaries: Step 2 moves from manual browser chat to programmatic API pipelines that chain 2 to 4 sequential LLM calls (~2,500 tokens total) using structured system instructions, few-shot templates, and output moderation checks, differing from Step 3 by relying strictly on data passed directly in the prompt rather than external vector retrieval. Concrete Tool Stack: Engineers wired the OpenAI ChatCompletion API (gpt-3.5-turbo-0613, gpt-4-0613), LangChain (PromptTemplate, LLMChain, SimpleSequentialChain, StructuredOutputParser), and Gradio for rapid UI demos. Use Cases & Context Switching: These patterns were taught to millions of engineers in Andrew Ng and Isa Fulford's landmark May 2023 courses ChatGPT Prompt Engineering for Developers and Building Systems with ChatGPT (implementing classification-then-response chains and moderation guardrails); engineers used Step 2 for deterministic text processing and switched to Step 3 whenever reference documents exceeded 3,000 words. Step Resource Use: A single task processes $\sim 2,500$ tokens across chained calls, $\sim 1.0 \times 10^{14}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.005\text{ USD}$ (on gpt-3.5-turbo), $1.0\text{ Wh}$, and $8\text{ minutes}$ of human prompt debugging versus $6.0\text{ seconds}$ of AI execution, accounting for $20\%$ of users, $25\%$ of tokens, and $20\%$ of era spend.Step 3 — Custom Retrieval-Augmented Generation (L3-P3): Step Boundaries: Step 3 splits private documents (PDFs, Notion wikis, SQL tables) into 500-token chunks, converts them into numerical vectors (embeddings), stores them in a vector database, and injects the top-$k$ matching chunks (~6,000 tokens across retrieval and synthesis) into an LLM prompt, solving Step 1 and Step 2's memory cutoff and hallucination on private data, while failing when questions require synthesizing an entire 500-page book or executing live actions in external software. Concrete Tool Stack: Developers combined LangChain (DocumentLoaders, RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter, RetrievalQA), LlamaIndex (created by Jerry Liu), OpenAI text-embedding-ada-002 or Cohere Embeddings, and vector databases Pinecone, Chroma, Weaviate, Qdrant, and FAISS paired with GPT-4. Use Cases & Context Switching: Custom RAG was the #1 enterprise engineering pattern in Menlo Ventures' 2023 report ($1.7B total market) and the centerpiece of Harrison Chase's LangChain: Chat with Your Data and Jerry Liu's Building and Evaluating Advanced RAG courses on DeepLearning.AI; companies built internal "Chat with your corporate PDFs" portals at Step 3 while using Step 1 for general brainstorming. Step Resource Use: A single task processes $\sim 6,000$ tokens (embedding + top-$k$ context + GPT-4 answer), $\sim 1.5 \times 10^{15}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.12\text{ USD}$ (at GPT-4 $30/$60 per 1M prices), $15.0\text{ Wh}$, and $15\text{ minutes}$ of human chunking and retrieval tuning versus $12.0\text{ seconds}$ of pipeline latency, serving $10\%$ of users while generating $18\%$ of token volume and $25\%$ of era spend.Step 4 — Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT / LoRA, L3-P4): Step Boundaries: Step 4 freezes the base weights of an open-weight 7B–70B model (Llama-2 or Mistral-7B) and trains tiny low-rank adapter matrices (LoRA) in 4-bit precision (QLoRA) on a single 24GB–80GB GPU, providing full data privacy, custom domain formatting, and low inference cost compared to Step 3 GPT-4 calls, but unable to dynamically fetch real-time facts without still pairing with a Step 3 RAG pipeline. Concrete Tool Stack: Open-source practitioners deployed Meta Llama-2-7b-hf and 70b, Mistral-7B-v0.1, HuggingFace peft, bitsandbytes (Tim Dettmers' 4-bit NF4), trl (SFTTrainer), Axolotl, and vLLM for high-throughput serving on AWS or RunPod A100 GPUs. Use Cases & Context Switching: Healthcare, defense, and financial engineering teams prohibited from sending customer data to OpenAI APIs—plus open-source developers training specialized SQL-generation or medical-coding adapters—switched to Step 4 whenever Step 3 API costs or privacy compliance blocked deployment. Step Resource Use: A single task (amortizing a 2-hour $5 QLoRA training run over deployment queries) processes $\sim 4,000$ tokens, $\sim 5.6 \times 10^{14}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$0.04\text{ USD}$, $8.0\text{ Wh}$, and $45\text{ minutes}$ of dataset formatting and evaluation versus $15.0\text{ seconds}$ of amortized GPU runtime, representing $3\%$ of users, $4\%$ of tokens, and $10\%$ of era spend.Step 5 — Autonomous ReAct Loops & Unconstrained Agents (L3-P5): Step Boundaries: Step 5 was Era 3's experimental apex—wrapping GPT-4 inside an open-ended while loop that prompted the model to generate a Thought, select an Action (web search, Python REPL, file write), read the Observation (ReAct pattern), and repeat 15 to 50 times (~45,000 tokens) until a high-level goal was met—differing from Steps 1–4 by granting the model autonomous control flow, but lacking reliable tool schemas and state checkpoints, which caused infinite loops and context overflow. Concrete Tool Stack: Early adopters experimented with AutoGPT (Significant Gravitas, the fastest-growing GitHub repository of Spring 2023), BabyAGI (Yohei Nakajima), AgentGPT, and LangChain AgentExecutor (zero-shot-react-description). Use Cases & Context Switching: Viral developer demos attempted to "conduct autonomous market research and build a website"; as LangChain CEO Harrison Chase later documented ("The first autonomous agents didn't work, why?"), a 90% per-step reliability rate compounded over 20 unconstrained steps drops to $0.90^{20} = 12.2\%$ success, forcing developers to abandon Step 5 after burning $50 in API credits and retreat to Step 2 and Step 3 chains. Step Resource Use: A single task consumes $\sim 45,000$ tokens across recursive GPT-4 context re-reads, $\sim 2.5 \times 10^{16}\text{ FLOPs}$, $\$1.80\text{ USD}$, $180.0\text{ Wh}$, and $25\text{ minutes}$ of human babysitting and aborting loops versus $120.0\text{ seconds}$ (2 minutes) of AI runtime, representing $2\%$ of users, $3\%$ of tokens, and $5\%$ of era spend.Full-Ladder Resource Totals (L3)Aggregate Era Footprint (2022-11 to 2023-11): Across its 12-month window, $L_3$ exploded to $\sim 180\text{ million}$ active users globally, logging $\sim 6.5\text{ billion}$ human hours. Era 4 Ladder (L4: 2023-11 to 2024-09) — Multimodal Context, Native Tool-Calling & Scaffolded HarnessesBottom-Line Summary: Era 4 (2023-11 to 2024-09) dismantled Era 3's custom engineering bottlenecks through three breakthroughs: 128k-to-2M token context windows with 50%–90% prompt caching discounts, native multimodal vision and structured JSON tool-calling, and the release of Claude 3.5 Sonnet (June 2024) which ignited multi-file AI code editors (Cursor). RAG Sinks into Chat UI: Simple single-document vector RAG (L3-P3) sunk directly into Step 1's paperclip file-upload button in ChatGPT Plus and Claude.ai. Why Spend Rises as Prices Fall: Despite API token prices falling over 99% for equivalent capability between 2020 and 2026, enterprise AI spending grew 21.7-fold from $1.7B (2023) to $37.0B (2025) because test-time reasoning and autonomous agent loops multiplied single-task token consumption from $10^2$ to $10^6+$ tokens.Pattern 1 — The 15-Month Step-Sinking Cycle: Advanced developer steps consistently migrate downward into the default consumer interface of the subsequent era. In Era 1 (2018–2020), classifying sentiment or extracting entities required Step 4 GPU fine-tuning of BERT; in Era 2 (2020–2022), that became a Step 2 zero-shot davinci prompt; by Era 3 (2022–2023), it sank into Step 1 free browser chat. Similarly, Era 3's signature engineering project—Step 3 custom Python + Pinecone PDF RAG—sank within 12 months into Era 4's Step 1 paperclip upload button (ChatGPT Plus and Claude 200k Projects), while Era 4's custom visualization scripts sank into Era 5's Step 1 Artifacts and MCP Apps. Consequently, startups and in-house enterprise teams that build thin wrappers around current context limits face rapid getting-outdated risk unless they anchor on proprietary workflow state or vertical domain integration.Pattern 2 — Jevons Paradox & Test-Time Compute: As Menlo Ventures explicitly observed in its 2025–2026 enterprise analysis, Jevons Paradox governs AI resource consumption: plunging per-token inference costs drive orders-of-magnitude expansions in total token demand. When GPT-3 davinci cost $60.00 per million tokens in 2020, a 1,200-token few-shot call ($0.072) was considered expensive, and a 250,000-token agent loop ($15.00) was commercially impossible. Once hardware efficiency (+49% performance/dollar/year), model distillation (Flash and Haiku tiers), and prompt caching (50%–90% discounts) dropped effective input costs toward $0.18–$0.75 per million tokens, developers unlocked Step 2 reasoning loops (35,000 tokens), Step 4 Claude Code repository sweeps (250,000 tokens), and Step 5 multi-agent GUI workflows (1,200,000 tokens), pushing single-task expenditure back up to $1.10–$6.50 per complex job.Pattern 3 — Verifiability Dictates Agent Autonomy: Comparing Epoch AI's compute trade-off laws with Anthropic's production engineering findings explains the uneven success of Step 4 and Step 5 agents across industries. Epoch AI established that spending 5 to 6 extra orders of magnitude of inference compute (pass@k and iterative refinement) yields massive capability gains only in domains where candidate solutions can be verified cheaply and automatically—specifically software coding (via unit tests and compilers) and formal mathematics. In coding, Claude Code or Cursor can run pytest via bash, inspect the exact traceback, and self-correct across 50 turns, propelling coding to $4.0 billion (55% of all departmental AI spend). Conversely, in ambiguous business workflows without automated verification tests, autonomous errors compound undetected, explaining why enterprises strongly prefer predefined Step 2 workflows or Step 3 MCP integrations and why 40%+ of unconstrained non-coding agent pilots risk cancellation.Pattern 4 — Shift of Human Effort from Execution to Specification & Audit: Tracking the ratio of human minutes to AI runtime seconds across the five ladders reveals a profound workplace reversal. In Era 1 and Era 2, human practitioners spent 10 to 60 minutes preparing features or few-shot examples for an AI execution pass lasting 0.05 to 5.0 seconds. By Era 5 Steps 4 and 5, the human spends 8 to 20 minutes upfront writing a crisp architectural specification (CLAUDE.md or design document) and reviewing the final pull request diff, while the AI executes autonomously for 240 to 900 seconds (4 to 15 minutes) across hundreds of file reads and test runs.4.2.
